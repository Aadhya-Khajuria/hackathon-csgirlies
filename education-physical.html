Overview and Tips:

What is Physical Health?

Physical health is basically how well your body's holding up and doing what it's supposed to, being able to get through your day without wearing yourself out or straining yourself. It ties into things like eating right, staying active, and staving off illness, and it's a big part of feeling good overall and having a solid quality of life. Put simply, it's when everything inside and outside your body, organs, tissues, cells, all of it, is working the way it should. That means you can go about your daily life without limitations.

The Connection of Physical and Mental Health

Mental health isn't just about emotions or thoughts. Changes in mental wellbeing can affect things like sleep, energy, heart rate, digestion, immune function, physical activity, and daily habits. At the same time, physical health problems can also affect someone's mental wellbeing.

Why can you improve your mental and physical health through movement?

Exercise does more than shape your body—it rewards your brain. Moving releases dopamine, a feel-good chemical that naturally lifts your spirits. This creates a positive feedback loop: the more you move, the happier you feel. Aiming for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week keeps your mind strong.

The role of sleep

A quality sleep routine supports physical recovery, optimises cognitive function, and strengthens the human immune system. During deep sleep, the body is heavily influenced by the parasympathetic nervous system (PSNS). This network of nerves helps calm the body by slowing the heart rate and lowering blood pressure, thereby allowing tissue repair. Not receiving adequate amounts of sleep each night puts severe stress on physiological systems, which can lead to chronic illnesses over the long term.

Dietary Changes: What to expect:

Ever notice how your diet affects your daily mood? Eating a balanced mix of proteins, fats, and carbs supports your mind, as these are macromolecules crucial to our psychological processes. Healthy eating isn't about cutting out your favourite "bad" foods. When you take control of your plate, you build emotional strength.
